---
title: "Payment Safety for Servers"
description: "When to start paid work, handle expiring exact payments, and use funded channels for metered requests."
---

Choose payment ordering before starting costly work. A successful `/verify`
validates an authorization at that moment; it does not reserve the customer's
funds or guarantee that a later `/settle` will succeed.

| Payment flow | Before starting work | After work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `exact` for inference, streaming or other costly work | Verify the payment against your server's terms, settle it, and confirm successful payment | Deliver the result; handle service failures under your refund policy |
| Funded `batch-settlement` / payment channel | Confirm channel funding, validate its state and authorization, and atomically reserve enough capacity for this request | Persist the actual charge/voucher and redeem on the channel's schedule |

## Exact payments: confirm before inference or streaming

An attacker can sign an authorization that expires shortly after verification.
If your server verifies, runs inference, streams the result, and only then tries
to collect, the payment may already have expired. Keeping a sufficient token
balance does not prevent this attack.

For costly exact-payment endpoints:

1. Match the payment to your server-controlled network, asset, recipient, amount
   and resource requirements. Do not let the client choose the terms you verify.
2. Call `/verify` and require `isValid: true`.
3. Immediately call `/settle` with that same payment and requirements. Wait for
   the confirmed successful outcome before starting inference, irreversible
   effects, or any paid response bytes.
4. If the outcome is pending or unknown, keep the application operation pending
   too. Preserve the original payload and transaction identity, then follow
   [settlement recovery](/x402/facilitators/capacity-and-limits).

Use your SDK's documented upfront settlement flow when it supports it, or
implement this ordering explicitly. Middleware that waits to settle until after
your handler runs can protect delivery of a buffered response while still
leaving you with unpaid inference cost. Check when the handler executes, not
just when response bytes leave the server.

An HTTP 200, a transaction hash, successful simulation, or a broadcast
acknowledgment alone is not a confirmed payment. `settlement_pending`,
`duplicate_settlement`, and a lost response require reconciliation. Do not
start a replacement payment merely because the original authorization expired:
the original transfer may already have landed.

## EVM: use the signed expiry

For EIP-3009, inspect the signed `authorization.validBefore`. For Permit2, inspect
the signed `permit2Authorization.deadline` and any required sponsored approval's
expiry. `maxTimeoutSeconds` in your requirements is not proof that the client
signed that much remaining lifetime.

PayAI's default EVM verification buffer is approximately six seconds. This is a
settlement buffer, not a promise that an authorization will survive your handler.
If you enforce a longer resource-specific margin, allow for verification,
queueing, transaction inclusion and clock skew, and configure compatible clients.
A 120-second floor is useful defense in depth, but does not secure a longer
stream or reserve the wallet's balance. Confirm exact payment before costly work.

## Solana: validity now is not remaining lifetime

PayAI verifies the original signed blockhash through simulation. An expired
blockhash is rejected, but a nearly expired one may still pass.
[`isBlockhashValid`](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/http/isblockhashvalid) also returns
only current validity, not remaining lifetime.

For a resource-specific freshness policy:

1. Retain a trusted `{blockhash, lastValidBlockHeight}` pair from
   [`getLatestBlockhash`](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/http/getlatestblockhash) or a
   trusted facilitator response.
2. Decode the signed transaction and match its actual blockhash to that stored
   pair. An unsigned client-supplied height is not authoritative.
3. Compare the stored height with [`getBlockHeight`](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/http/getblockheight)
   from a healthy RPC at consistent commitment. Require enough remaining blocks
   for your settlement path. Reject unknown or stale hashes under this policy
   and obtain a fresh signed payment only when no prior payment is unresolved.

Do not use a newly fetched hash's expiry for a different transaction, compare
slots to block heights, or assume a fixed lifetime in seconds. PayAI's
`/supported` blockhash hints do not force clients to use them; a freshness policy
must check the actual signed hash. Replacing that hash invalidates the payer's
signature. For exact payments, confirm settlement before work:
[`sendTransaction`](https://solana.com/docs/rpc/http/sendtransaction) only
acknowledges submission.

## Funded channels: reserve before work, redeem afterward

Funded batch settlement does not defer a separate EIP-3009 transfer or signed
Solana transaction for every resource request. Its per-request vouchers avoid
this specific authorization/blockhash expiry issue, so the exact-payment advice
to settle each request before inference does **not** apply to funded vouchers.

The initial deposit or a top-up can still expire or fail. Confirm funding before
treating it as spendable channel capacity. Validate the channel's current state,
reserve request capacity atomically across replicas, retain authorization and
voucher records, and redeem within withdrawal grace. Pending funding, an
exhausted channel, or a closing channel is not permission to keep serving.

Follow the [Solana batch guide](/x402/servers/batch-settlement) or
[EVM batch guide](/x402/servers/evm-batch-settlement) for metered charges, durable
storage, payout and closure. Internal batching of several `exact` transfers into
one on-chain transaction does not turn them into funded payment channels.
